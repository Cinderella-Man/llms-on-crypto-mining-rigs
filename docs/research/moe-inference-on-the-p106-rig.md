# Fitting 2026's MoE models onto nine P106s

*Research synthesis as of 2026-10-22. Options and claims only: nothing here is a decision, and no number below has been measured on this rig unless it is labelled as a rig fact.*

This report is a literature and source survey, compiled in October 2026, of which Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models and inference engines could run on the Manli M-P106L9-N6G mining rig: nine NVIDIA P106-090 6 GB cards, each on a PCIe 1.0 x1 link, behind a 2-core Celeron host ([hardware spec](../mining_rig.md)). It turns published benchmarks, model cards, source code and issue threads into 82 claims to test and an 81-experiment backlog. Measured results are published separately in [docs/benchmarks/](../benchmarks/), and the claims are being tested one by one.

**Status of measurements.** The rest of the report is the survey as compiled before any measurement. These claims have been tested on the rig so far ([single-card calibration](../benchmarks/2026-10-22-single-card-calibration.md); [small-MoE overhead](../benchmarks/2026-10-22-small-moe-overhead.md)):

- **C1:** a P106-090 decodes Llama-2-7B Q4_0 at ~19.5 tok/s, so the 16–22 tok/s alternative held ([calibration](../benchmarks/2026-10-22-single-card-calibration.md)).
- **C2:** confirmed; pp512 is ~218 tok/s ([calibration](../benchmarks/2026-10-22-single-card-calibration.md)).
- **C3:** confirmed for Q4_K_M, which uses 35 % of the measured memory bandwidth against 42 % for Q4_0 and 54 % for Q8_0 ([calibration](../benchmarks/2026-10-22-single-card-calibration.md)).
- **C4:** confirmed; llama.cpp disables CUDA graphs on Pascal at runtime ([small-MoE overhead](../benchmarks/2026-10-22-small-moe-overhead.md)).
- **C6:** decode of small MoEs is launch-bound (~10 µs per graph node) but mostly not CPU-bound: the GPU is busy 95–98 % of the time, and one busy CPU core costs at most 6 % ([small-MoE overhead](../benchmarks/2026-10-22-small-moe-overhead.md)).
- **C20:** memory runs at the full 4006 MHz in the P0 state under compute load ([calibration](../benchmarks/2026-10-22-single-card-calibration.md)).
- **C21:** not applicable on the tested install, where ASPM is already disabled on every link ([hardware spec](../mining_rig.md)).
- **C27:** confirmed on the tested install, which uses LUKS disk encryption: aes-xts decrypts at ~1088 MiB/s in memory, well above the SSD's ~485 MB/s.
- **C35 (second half):** confirmed; 1–1.5B-active MoEs run at 29–54 tok/s, far below their ceilings ([small-MoE overhead](../benchmarks/2026-10-22-small-moe-overhead.md)).

The rig can hold essentially the whole 2026 class of "30–36B total, ~3B active" Mixture-of-Experts models at Q4 entirely in VRAM, plus a handful of 75–140B "stretch" MoEs at 2–3 bits per weight, but today **only the llama.cpp family can drive them on Pascal sm_61 cards**. Mainline llama.cpp is the one engine with no known blocker. ik_llama.cpp is promising but its README requires an AVX2 CPU, which the Celeron lacks. **Maya, Strata and "infer" (ninfer) are infeasible as shipped**, for reasons that range from tensor-core-only kernels to 32 GB host-RAM floors. The notes' arithmetic puts batch-1 decode for ~3B-active models at a **57–100 tok/s bandwidth ceiling and a 20–50 tok/s planning range**, and ~10B-active stretch models such as Qwen3.5-122B-A10B at ~3 bpw at **roughly 13–24 tok/s**. The reasons: layer split reads one card at a time, and a 2-core Celeron must launch ~1,500–2,000 kernels per token because **CUDA graphs are switched off on Pascal** in current llama.cpp. None of these numbers has been measured on a P106-090. **No published LLM benchmark exists for this 5-SM card**, so the first job is calibration, not model shopping. **The ~26 GB of free disk on the tested install, not the 54 GB of VRAM, is the first wall.** That makes keeping the model library on a separate, more powerful PC on the same LAN (a "workstation"), and copying one GGUF at a time to the rig or mounting the library read-only over NFS, the backbone of every experiment. The rest of this report lays out the constraints and what they rule in and out, the model and engine landscape, ten themes of ideas, 82 numbered claims to test, an 81-experiment backlog in five phases with dependencies and go-ahead flags, and a reusable baseline protocol and results schema.

## Nine 6 GB pools on single PCIe lanes set every rule

The rig is nine P106-090 cards: GP106 dies with **5 SMs each, ~6068 MiB usable, ~173 GB/s measured bandwidth, FP16 at 1/64 of the FP32 rate, dp4a INT8 at 4× FP32 and no tensor cores**. Each card sits on a **PCIe 1.0 x1** link behind two ASM1184e switches with **no peer-to-peer access**, so every GPU-to-GPU byte crosses host memory. One GPU at a time uploads at about 189 MB/s, and all nine together reach about 715 MB/s ([public hardware spec](../mining_rig.md)). The host is a 2-core 1.8 GHz Celeron 3865U with SSE4.2 and AES-NI but no AVX, AVX2 or FMA. It has 3.7 GiB of usable RAM (about 1.1 GiB used at idle under GNOME, one SO-DIMM slot free, a 3.7 GiB swap file) and a LUKS2-encrypted 64 GB SSD with ~26 GB free. The tested install unlocks that disk with a passphrase only, so every reboot needs someone at the rig's keyboard to type it. The toolkit is CUDA 12.9.2 on the 580 driver branch, which is the last pairing that supports Pascal ([llama.cpp discussion #25978](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/25978); [ik_llama.cpp docs/pascal-volta.md](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/blob/main/docs/pascal-volta.md)).

### Decode time is one card's bandwidth plus a CPU tax

With llama.cpp's default `--split-mode layer`, each GPU holds a contiguous slice of layers and the GPUs run one after another. Batch-1 decode is therefore capped by **one card's bandwidth divided by the bytes read per token**, however many cards are used ([llama.cpp docs/multi-gpu.md](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/multi-gpu.md)). The multi-GPU note turns this into a working model: **T_token ≈ max(T_gpu, T_launch) + T_graph_build + T_logits + T_sample + (G−1)·T_hop**. T_gpu is the active bytes divided by effective bandwidth. T_launch is about 1,500–1,900 graph nodes per token for a 48-layer MoE times 6–12 µs per node on this CPU without graphs. T_logits is ~2.9 ms for a 152k vocabulary over PCIe 1.0 x1, and T_hop is ~0.1 ms per host-staged GPU boundary. The per-node cost comes from a GTX 1080 desktop, where granite-3.1-3b-a800m ran at 81 tok/s without graphs and 124 tok/s with them, still not bandwidth-bound ([PR #27721](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27721)). On that basis **a 3B-active MoE is as much launch-bound as bandwidth-bound on this rig**, while a 10B-active model is clearly bandwidth-bound.

The bandwidth half carries its own uncertainty. Pascal GDDR5 cards reach 50–69 % of spec bandwidth in llama.cpp decode: a P106-100 does 30.6 tok/s on Llama-2-7B Q4_0, about 117 GB/s effective ([llama.cpp discussion #15013](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/15013)). To match that, a 5-SM P106-090 would have to pull **~23 GB/s per SM, against ≤13 GB/s per SM on every Pascal card with published numbers**. Whether MMVQ can keep enough loads in flight on 5 SMs is the single most important unmeasured fact about this rig (claim C1).

## Thirty-odd MoEs fit the VRAM, but 26 GB of disk is the first wall

The 2026 field has converged on a **"30–36B total / ~3B active" class**: Qwen3/3.5/3.6, the Nemotron-H 30B-A3B family, GLM-4.7-Flash, North-Mini-Code, Trinity-Mini, kanana-2, Tencent Hy and, at 3.8B active, Gemma 4 26B-A4B. At Q4 these produce **14–25 GB files**, which fit both the 26 GB of free disk and the ~50 GB of usable VRAM with room for long context ([small-MoE note](notes/models_small_moe.md)). The newest Qwen generation, Qwen3.8, has no small MoE. Its MoEs are Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (~125B core plus a 51B n-gram table) and a 2.4T model, so **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B is the closest MoE relative to the dense Qwen3.8-27B that has already run on this rig**. It shares the Gated DeltaNet layers that model already exercised on these cards ([Qwen HF listing](https://huggingface.co/Qwen); [Qwen3.6-35B-A3B config](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B/blob/main/config.json)).

Attention design matters more than MoE shape on 6 GB cards, because it sets the KV-cache cost on every GPU. Per token at f16, **Nemotron-H and Ling-3.0-tiny need ~6–7 KiB, the Qwen3.5/3.6 hybrids and Gemma 4 20 KiB, GLM-4.7-Flash's MLA ~53 KiB, and plain-GQA Qwen3-30B-A3B 96 KiB**. A llama.cpp contributor independently confirms the 20,480-byte figure for both Gemma 4 and Qwen 35B-A3B ([PR #28450](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/28450)). Among the stretch models the spread is wider: GLM-4.5-Air needs 184 KiB per token and MiniMax-M2 248 KiB, which caps their context on this rig. A second number matters as much as total size: the **always-on share** of each token's reads (attention, SSM, shared experts, LM head). For Qwen3.6-35B-A3B about 1.94B of the ~2.95B weights read per token are always-on, and the 248k-vocabulary LM head alone is 0.51B. Quant recipes that keep those tensors at 6–8 bits therefore cost more speed than their average bits-per-weight suggests (claim C37).

### What the published quality numbers can and cannot tell

Vendor tables put the Qwen3.6-35B-A3B lineage on top of the ~3B-active class, with **SWE-bench Verified 73.4 and Terminal-Bench 2.0 51.5** ([Qwen3.6 card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B)). They also show how unreliable cross-card comparisons are: Gemma 4 26B-A4B scores **17.4, 35.8 or 57.4 on SWE-bench Verified** depending on whose harness ran it ([Qwen card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B); [KAT-Coder card](https://huggingface.co/Kwaipilot/KAT-Coder-V2.5-Dev); [Lightning card](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Lightning-30B-A3B-BF16)). Artificial Analysis rescaled its index at least twice in 2026 (the same model scored 43, then 32, then 18), so its numbers compare only within one snapshot ([AA on X](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2049881951260283097); [AA model page](https://artificialanalysis.ai/models/qwen3-6-35b-a3b)). For a 20–40 tok/s rig, token efficiency may matter more than rank: AA measured **~3.5 minutes per index task for Qwen3.6-35B-A3B against ~0.5 minutes for Nemotron 3.5 Lightning** ([AA Lightning article](https://artificialanalysis.ai/articles/nemotron-3-5-lightning-launch)).

The "big model at low bits versus small model at high bits" question is genuinely open. At full precision, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B already matches Qwen3.5-122B-A10B on coding (SWE-bench Verified 73.4 vs 72.0; LiveCodeBench 80.4 vs 78.9). The 122B leads on knowledge (MMLU-Pro 86.7 vs 85.2; HLE 25.3 vs 21.4) ([Qwen3.5-122B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-122B-A10B)). At the ~43 GiB that fits here, the 122B sits near 3 bpw, where ubergarm's IQ2_KL loses 5.9 % in perplexity ([ubergarm](https://huggingface.co/ubergarm/Qwen3.5-122B-A10B-GGUF)). Expert pruning offers another route: REAP keeps coding within ~1–3 points at 25 % expert removal but drops multiple-choice averages ~13 % at 50 % ([REAP paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13999v2)). An independent 2026 benchmark found pruning costs far more perplexity than Q4 quantisation (+27 % at 20 % pruning) ([MoEXBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21693v1)). No published same-memory head-to-head exists for these specific models, which makes it one of the most valuable things this rig could measure.

## Only the llama.cpp family runs on sm_61 today

**Mainline llama.cpp still compiles and runs on sm_61 with a CUDA 12.x toolkit, but nobody upstream tests it.** No source announces Pascal's removal. CUDA 13 cannot target sm_61, and ggml's CMake adds the `61-virtual` architecture only for toolkits older than 13 ([ggml-cuda CMakeLists.txt](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/ggml/src/ggml-cuda/CMakeLists.txt)). Recent Pascal bug reports sit unanswered: the MMVQ fusion gate that misses cc 6.1 ([#30334](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/30334)) and the Pascal MMQ tile configuration ([#30235](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/30235)). Downstream packagers are dropping the architecture: LM Studio 0.4.21 reportedly no longer runs sm_61 ([lmstudio #2316](https://github.com/lmstudio-ai/lmstudio-bug-tracker/issues/2316)), and Docker Model Runner excludes it ([model-runner #929](https://github.com/docker/model-runner/issues/929)). On sm_61, matmuls use MMVQ for 1–8 tokens and dp4a MMQ above that. Flash attention uses FP32 vec/tile kernels, because cc 610 is explicitly marked as lacking fast FP16 ([ggml-cuda common.cuh](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/ggml/src/ggml-cuda/common.cuh)). The rig's build (commit 85d5703a3, 2026-09-04, graphs/FA/NCCL/NATIVE on, RPC off) predates v0.5.0 (2026-09-23) and v0.6.0 (2026-10-05). The newest nightly on 2026-10-22 is b11815 ([releases](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/releases)). Several candidates need something newer than the rig's build: LFM2.5-24B (quantized with b11606), the Nemotron MTP extension (2026-09-17) and very likely Nemotron Bolt. Pascal kernel tables were restructured between June and October 2026 ([pascallama.cpp](https://github.com/madsteph74/pascallama.cpp)), so pinning a known-good commit and benchmarking before every bump is part of the method, not an afterthought.

**ik_llama.cpp is the most interesting fork and the most uncertain one.** It offers `-sm graph` tensor/graph parallelism, fused MoE, the IQ_K/KS/KL/KT quant families, Smart Expert Reduction (`-ser`) and MTP ([ik parameters.md](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/blob/main/docs/parameters.md)). A contributor landed a run of Pascal PRs in September–October 2026. IQ4_K MMVQ more than doubled P40 decode (31.75 to 67.88 tok/s) ([ik PR #2509](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/pull/2509)), a head-512 flash-attention path took P40 Gemma-4-12B prefill from 67 to 386 tok/s ([ik PR #2647](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/pull/2647)), and head-256 prefill moved to a tile_f32 kernel (246 to 449 tok/s on a P40) ([ik PR #2580](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/pull/2580)). Against that, **the README requires AVX2 for the CPU side and lists "AVX-only CPUs" as unsupported**, and the Celeron has no AVX at all ([ik PR #2670](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/pull/2670)). Its sm_61 placement was derived on a P100 shimmed to report cc 6.1, and some flash-attention and KV-type combinations fall back to the CPU. In ik, gpt-oss with a q8_0 cache decodes on the CPU ([ik pascal-volta.md](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/blob/main/docs/pascal-volta.md)). Whether ik builds and starts at all on this host is the gate for every ik experiment.

Around these two sit Pascal patch sets (pascallama.cpp's 96-row MMQ tiles, key-sh's P100 patches including a pre-Volta AllReduce, shinbunbun's tensor-mode plus MTP patches), the Vulkan backend and the RPC backend. On a P106-100 the Vulkan backend matches CUDA on decode but reaches only ~45 % of its prefill ([Vulkan scoreboard #10879](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/10879)). It is a hedge against the CUDA 12 toolchain ageing out. The RPC backend is a self-described "fragile and insecure" proof of concept that nonetheless lets a workstation stream weights to remote GPUs with no model file on the rig ([RPC README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/rpc/README.md)). Among wrappers, llama-swap and llama-server's router mode keep every llama.cpp flag available. Ollama ships a CUDA 12 runner that includes compute 6.1 but hides `-ot`/`-ts` placement ([localaimaster](https://localaimaster.com/blog/pascal-gpus-after-cuda-13); [Ollama GPU docs](https://docs.ollama.com/gpu)).

### Maya, Strata and "infer": three engine names checked one by one

Each name was first matched to a project, then checked against the rig.

**"Maya" is almost certainly mw00/project-maya** (confidence ~85 %), an engine for **GLM-5.3-Flash (321B) and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 (748B)**. It needs compute capability 7.0+ ("Pascal … is experimental, not validated yet"), 32 GB of RAM (it pins all but ~6 GB for its expert tier), fast NVMe, and a 94.7 GB minimum model ([project-maya README](https://github.com/mw00/project-maya)). It fails here on four independent grounds: GPU generation, RAM, disk and model size, the last being nearly twice the rig's VRAM.

**"Strata" is almost certainly Niko1221/Strata** (confidence ~90 %, 30.2k stars). It runs Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (125B) and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B split across GPU, RAM and SSD ([Strata](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata)). It is the only 2026-wave engine with a real Pascal path: an experimental CUDA 12.9 sm_60/61 engine, community P40 reports of 37–38 tok/s at IQ3_S, and a layer split that needs no P2P and accepts x1 slots ([OLDER_GPUS.md](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/blob/main/docs/OLDER_GPUS.md); [MULTI_GPU.md](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/blob/main/docs/MULTI_GPU.md)). As shipped it is blocked here several times over. It wants RAM equal to "the experts + about 10 GB", computes uncached experts on the CPU with AVX2/AVX-512 kernels, keeps a 29 GB n-gram shard on the SSD, turns 8 GB-or-smaller cards "Off", and auto-places across at most four cards ([MODELS.md](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/blob/main/docs/MODELS.md)). A nine-way split in low-RAM mode with every expert in VRAM is *conceivable*, because its IQ2_XS Qwen3.8 build needs 39.2 GB. That is a hack test, not a supported path (E4.7).

**"infer" is ninfer (Neroued/ninfer).** It is a one-person, from-scratch C++/CUDA engine for a single RTX 5090. Its build rejects every architecture except `sm_120a`, and it has no multi-GPU ([ninfer README](https://github.com/Neroued/ninfer)). It is the only "-infer" engine that 2026 threads and roundups put next to Strata ([HN 49958120](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49958120); [carteakey.dev](https://carteakey.dev/blog/local-inference/the-rise-of-overfit-inference-engines/)), and Strata credits it ([HOW_IT_WORKS.md](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata/blob/main/docs/HOW_IT_WORKS.md)). Its broadest fork, iamwavecut/ninfer-all, reaches down to Volta. It runs Gemma 4 26B-A4B and Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and has an eight-stage pipeline through pinned host memory without P2P, which is close to what this rig needs, but it states **"Pascal and older … no build"** ([ninfer-all](https://github.com/iamwavecut/ninfer-all)). Even its Volta tier runs attention and dense projections on FP16 tensor cores. A similarly named engine, deepseek-ai/DeepInfer, went public on 2026-10-20 and targets the same DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash checkpoint as Maya, but it needs SM100 or Ascend 950 GPUs, CUDA 13 and datacenter-scale expert parallelism ([DeepInfer README](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepInfer)). **Both are infeasible on sm_61 as shipped.** The only no-matrix-core port of ninfer (AMD gfx906) reached 0.52–0.66× of llama.cpp's speed before its author stopped, because ninfer's lead comes from cheap speculative verification on tensor cores ([ninfer-gfx906](https://github.com/JCraigWasTaken/ninfer-gfx906)).

## Ten themes hold every idea worth testing

Each idea below is phrased as something to try or a claim to check. The claim IDs (C1–C82) point to the master claims table and the experiment IDs (E0.1–E4.15) to the backlog.

### Models: start on known-good kernels, then probe one Pascal risk at a time

One natural sequence runs from mature kernels to new ones. **Qwen3-30B-A3B or Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B** is a plain-GQA control that measures the rig's efficiency factor (E1.1). **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B** follows, since its Gated DeltaNet layers already ran here inside Qwen3.8-27B (C36, E1.4). After that, each model carries one distinctive Pascal risk: Nemotron-H's Mamba2 scan on 5-SM cards (C38, E1.8), Gemma 4's head-512 attention (C40, E1.9), GLM-4.7-Flash's MLA (C39, E1.10), gpt-oss-20b's MXFP4 plus attention sinks (C41, E1.11) and LFM2.5's short convolutions (C42, E1.12). Testing them one at a time isolates which kernel family behaves badly. Fine-tunes that share a validated architecture, such as KAT-Coder-V2.5-Dev on the Qwen3.6 layout, cost only a download once the base works (E1.13). Wall-clock usefulness is its own idea: a model that thinks less can finish answers sooner at 20–40 tok/s despite a lower benchmark rank (C43, E1.15). For the stretch tier, the cheapest entry points need no disk workaround: Nemotron Bolt "24GB" at 22.02 GB (E3.1) and Qwen3-Coder-Next at IQ2 (24.96 GB) (E3.2). Qwen3.5-122B, the GLM-Air pair, Mistral Small 4, Laguna and the REAP checkpoints all need NFS or a USB SSD (E3.3–E3.7). Several small models can also stay resident at once, for example gpt-oss-20b on three cards next to Qwen3.6 UD-IQ4_XS on six, which matters if throughput ever becomes a goal (E2.10).

### Quants and compression: bytes per token decide speed, but which bytes matters

On a bandwidth-bound rig, speed should scale with bytes read per token. But for Unsloth "UD" files the always-on tensors (attention, DeltaNet, shared experts, LM head) sit at 5–8 bits, so dropping from UD-Q4_K_XL to UD-IQ3_XXS might buy only ~1.1× rather than ~1.7× (C37). A quant ladder on one model separates effective bandwidth from fixed per-token overhead (E2.5). Format choice is a second axis. Unsloth measured I-quants 5–10 % slower than K-quants on its GPU ([Unsloth Qwen3.5 benchmarks](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3.5/gguf-benchmarks)), and on 5-SM cards the extra unpacking work could make K- and IQ-quants issue-bound where Q4_0 and Q8_0 are not (C3, C55). Unsloth also retired MXFP4 from its mixed recipes as worse than Q4_K on attention and SSM tensors ([same source](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/qwen3.5/gguf-benchmarks)). Uploader labels say little about the routed-expert bits inside: Unsloth's "UD-IQ3_XXS" for Qwen3.5-122B keeps attention at Q6_K with gate/up at IQ2_S and down at IQ3_XXS, per the large-MoE note's header reads. That opens a rig-specific custom mix built with `llama-quantize --tensor-type-file` on the workstation, sized so each six-layer block fills ~5.3–5.5 GiB per card (C57, E3.11). Rarely routed experts get too little imatrix calibration, a known MoE problem ([EAQuant](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13329v3)). For compression beyond quantization, the options are REAP and REAM checkpoints (C51, E3.4), DIY pruning with Cerebras' layer-wise observer on the workstation (E3.12), a static expert-count override (`--override-kv <arch>.expert_used_count=int:N`, confirmed working in [#19528](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/19528)) (C52, E2.8), ik's `-ser` (C53) and mainline's `--prune-layers` (E3.13). On KV cache, MoEXBench found Q8/Q8 essentially lossless and Q4/Q4 about +1.8 % perplexity ([MoEXBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21693v1)), which feeds the KV sweep (E1.5).

### Engines and forks: pin mainline, gate ik, treat the rest as long shots

Mainline at two pinned commits, the rig's 2026-09-04 build and a current nightly, gives a "build delta" that separates upstream changes from rig changes (E1.2). Pascal-specific patches (the PR #27721 graphs gate, the #30334 fusion gate, the #30235 Q8_0 tile, pascallama's 96-row tiles, key-sh's q4_K dp4a reuse) are cheap A/B builds (E2.1, E2.3). Testing whether an official Linux CUDA 12.8 nightly runs on sm_61 and the no-AVX CPU could remove local builds entirely (C32, E2.14). ik_llama.cpp is worth a one-hour gate test (C62, E2.11) before any deeper work (E2.12), because its IQ4_K Pascal MMVQ (C56) and `-sm graph` (C63) are the only fork-level ideas with Pascal evidence behind them. The Vulkan backend is a hedge (C64, E2.13). Outside the llama.cpp family, the short list of things that could plausibly start is fastllm (E4.3), a vLLM-Pascal fork (E4.4), MLC-LLM (E4.5), mistral.rs with candle's pre-Volta patch (E4.6), and hacked runs of Strata and colibri (E4.7, E4.8). Every one first depends on platform probes for torch, RAM and SIMD (E4.1). For ninfer, a compile probe (E4.10) and a run on a tensor-core workstation GPU such as an sm_120 card (E4.11) settle whether a Pascal port is worth weeks of work. Its design documents (memory-balanced stage solver, per-card route calibration, n-gram copy drafting) can be mined for ideas to try inside llama.cpp at no cost (E4.13).

### Multi-GPU split modes and placement: fewer, fuller cards versus more room

Layer split is the baseline. Its bandwidth ceiling does not change with card count, so **using the fewest cards that hold the model plus KV** saves only the hop and scheduler costs (~2–8 % by the notes' estimate, C7). It does free cards for a second instance or a draft model. More cards buy context and higher-bit quants (E1.3). llama.cpp's default split divides by layer count or free memory, not by bytes, so models with uneven layers (Bolt, GLM-Air's 1.4 GiB MTP layer, Ling-3.0-flash's 1.65 GiB last layer) need hand-made `-ts` or `-ot` maps near the limit (C47). ik warns against putting a layer's up and gate experts on different GPUs ([ik parameters.md](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/blob/main/docs/parameters.md)). `-sm tensor` is expected to lose at batch 1, at 0.3–0.7× of layer split, because each token needs ~96 host-staged all-reduces and N× the kernel launches. PR #19378's own numbers show qwen35moe 35B-A3B at 0.69× on two PCIe 4.0 x16 cards ([PR #19378](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/19378)), and four P100s ran Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at 39.6 tok/s in tensor mode against 61.4 in layer mode without graphs ([PR #27721](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27721)) (C10). Tensor mode also faces an internal AllReduce that refuses pre-Volta GPUs and more than two devices ([key-sh/p100-llama-cpp](https://github.com/key-sh/p100-llama-cpp)) (C11). One untried idea is that a cross-switch pair could beat a same-switch pair in tensor mode, because the switch uplink is not shared (E2.9). Expert parallelism has no llama.cpp implementation. A microbenchmark of host-staged broadcast and gather (E0.11) can say "no" before anyone writes code (C12). For throughput, slots in one instance amortise launches better than extra instances on two CPU cores. MoE batching gains are sublinear, because the experts touched per layer grow as 1 − (1 − k/E)^B (23 % at B=4 and 40 % at B=8 for 128/8), per the multi-GPU note (E2.10).

### Speculative decoding and MTP: the one decode accelerator with Pascal evidence

MTP lifted a single P40 from 37.9 to 51.6 tok/s on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B at `--spec-draft-n-max 2`, with depth 3 and above giving less ([#25978](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/25978)). On this rig each verify step still walks all the cards in the split, and extra draft tokens touch more distinct experts, so the gain is expected to be smaller (C59, E2.6). Two open bugs warn about correctness: greedy output diverging from vanilla ([#25618](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/25618)), and "////" loops after long sessions on Qwen3.6-27B ([#23577](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/23577)). Every MTP run therefore needs an output-equality check. Draftless n-gram modes cost no VRAM and suit repetitive code edits, but the docs warn that "MoE models need long drafts" ([speculative.md](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/docs/speculative.md)) (C60, E2.7). A small draft model on a spare card via `-devd` is another option, as are the Nemotron DSpark/DFlash drafters and Gemma 4's separate "-assistant" model. Pascal decode stays on MMVQ up to 8 columns, so drafts of ≤7 tokens avoid switching kernel families ([ik pascal-volta.md](https://github.com/ikawrakow/ik_llama.cpp/blob/main/docs/pascal-volta.md)). Speculation should also pay off more over RPC, because it amortises network round trips across accepted tokens (C61, E3.20).

### Distributed and RPC with the workstation: capacity, not speed

For a layer split across the network, the per-token payload is only 8–28 KiB, so latency rather than bandwidth sets the cost: ~0.3–4 ms per token for one crossing in and one out (C75). Schemes that cross the network every layer cost ~25–250 ms per token and lose to the rig alone. That covers attention on the workstation GPU with experts on the rig, expert parallelism and tensor parallelism across hosts (C77). Within that frame the ideas to test are: one `rpc-server` exposing all nine cards versus nine servers (C76, E3.17); keeping the output layer on the workstation so 594 KiB of logits never cross the wire (E3.16); the workstation GPU joining as a tenth device, for ~62 GB of VRAM with an 8 GB card (C78, E3.18); and the plain-LAN route versus a WireGuard-based mesh VPN, about 0.2–0.3 ms versus about 2 ms RTT (C74, E0.2). RPC makes the rig's disk and page cache irrelevant, because weights stream from the client. The cost is a 6–10 minute load for 45 GB over gigabit (C79) and an unauthenticated port that has had remote-code-execution bugs in 2026 ([PR #20908](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/20908)). Running it as a non-root user with a firewall allow-list, or behind an SSH tunnel, is part of the experiment (E3.14).

### Storage and loading: the loader, not the disk, may set load time

llama.cpp uploads tensors in one sequential loop with no per-GPU threads ([llama-model-loader.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/master/src/llama-model-loader.cpp)). If that holds, load time is capped by one PCIe 1.0 x1 link at ~190 MB/s (C23), and an NFS mount at ~113 MB/s costs ~1.7× the local load time rather than ~4× (C24). That makes "no local copy at all" far more viable than it first looks. The ideas to test are local copy, read-only NFS, USB 3.0 SSD and NBD, each with `-lm mmap`, `-lm none` and `-lm dio` (E0.6). Direct I/O is reported as pathological on network shares ([discussion #18758](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/18758)) (C25). With mmap, the CPU-resident token-embedding table stays backed by the remote file, so the share must stay mounted, while `-lm none` should let it go (C26). AES-NI makes the LUKS layer and AES-GCM SSH ciphers unlikely bottlenecks (C27, C28). A loader patch that uploads to all nine cards in parallel could approach the measured ~715 MB/s aggregate, ~3–4× faster for big models. No engine does this today, so it is an idea, not a feature. Hashing every file once on the workstation, against Hugging Face's LFS sha256, avoids hashing 40 GB on two Celeron cores before each run.

### Build and deploy: one container recipe, no AVX, versioned directories

Builds run on the workstation in `nvidia/cuda:12.9.2-devel-ubuntu24.04`, whose glibc 2.39 matches the rig's Ubuntu 24.04. A workstation with a CUDA 13 toolkit cannot build for Pascal at all, and building natively with CUDA 12.9 on a newer distribution (glibc ≥2.41, such as Ubuntu 26.04) risks the CUDA 12.9 / glibc header conflict ([#25978](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/25978)) (C33). The trap to avoid is that with `GGML_NATIVE=OFF`, ggml's x86 SIMD options default *on* and the binary would SIGILL on the Celeron. The build must pass `-DGGML_AVX=OFF -DGGML_AVX2=OFF -DGGML_FMA=OFF -DGGML_F16C=OFF -DGGML_BMI2=OFF -DGGML_AVX512=OFF` explicitly ([ggml/CMakeLists.txt](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/ggml/CMakeLists.txt)) (C30). Then `objdump -d … | grep -c ymm` should report zero, and `objdump -T` should show no GLIBC symbols newer than 2.39. Dynamic linking against the rig's existing `/usr/local/cuda-12.9/lib64` keeps each build at tens of MB, so many builds can sit side by side in versioned directories with a `BUILDINFO.json` (E0.3). A current workstation GPU (for example an sm_120 card) cannot run sm_61 SASS. Workstation runs are "does it start" evidence only, never performance evidence ([llama-swap #1185](https://github.com/mostlygeek/llama-swap/issues/1185) shows a PTX path aborting where SASS would not). Parity with the rig-built binary at the same commit is the first proof the pipeline works (C31, E0.4). For serving, llama-swap needs its health-check timeout raised from 120 s to ~900 s for multi-minute loads ([llama-swap config](https://github.com/mostlygeek/llama-swap/blob/main/docs/config.example.yaml)). Binding llama-server to localhost and reaching it through an SSH tunnel avoids opening a LAN listener on the rig.

### Hardware and driver tuning: read first, change later, and mind the keyboard

Every tuning idea starts with read-only inspection (E0.1). Does CUDA's forced P2 state lower the memory clock on this mining VBIOS? Pascal GeForce cards drop memory clocks under P2 ([NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/remove-p2-forced-state-from-drivers/241998)), though the rig's 173 GB/s measurement suggests this one does not (C20). Are locked clocks supported? Reports say no on GP106 GeForce. Is ASPM active on the x1 links (C21)? Changes come later, as deliberate decisions. Headless mode should free a few hundred MB of RAM and the Xorg context on every card (C22, E2.15). A 60–75 W power-limit sweep should leave decode flat and cut prefill (C19, E2.16). Memory overclocking is the only lever that raises the decode ceiling. Mining profiles show +12–18 % Ethash on GP106 at around +400 (Windows units) / +800 (Linux units) ([Kryptex GTX 1060](https://www.kryptex.com/overclocking/nvidia-gtx-1060-samsung-medium-overclock)), but it risks silent numerical corruption and driver hangs that need a reboot, and on the tested install every reboot needs the passphrase at the keyboard (C18, E2.17). Hardware options outside software are a second SO-DIMM (dual channel and page cache; E2.19), a USB 3.0 SSD (C29), and a larger internal SSD. The last one requires cloning a LUKS/LVM install, and one Ubuntu user's grown LUKS root failed to boot ([Ubuntu Discourse](https://discourse.ubuntu.com/t/need-help-with-boot-after-running-out-of-space-in-luks-volume-copying-to-larger-hard-drive/75327)).

### Quality evaluation: KLD for "did it change", task subsets for "is it good"

Perplexity compares quants only within one model family. KL divergence against reference logits is the sensitive, cheap signal for "how far is this quant or engine from the reference" ([perplexity README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/perplexity/README.md)). A ~152k-vocab model at `-c 512` stores ~78 MB of logits per chunk, so 50 chunks is ~3.9 GB (C80). That file cannot sit on the rig next to a 22 GB model, so it would be read over NFS. A workstation with enough RAM for the Q8_0 of a ≤36B MoE (32–38 GB) can produce its reference logits. For 100B-class models the best available quant becomes the stated reference. Running the comparison side on the rig captures Pascal kernel numerics, which may differ from the workstation's (C81). Task suites (llama.cpp's `tool_bench.py`, IFEval, GSM8K, HumanEval+/MBPP+ subsets, an Aider polyglot slice) should run from the workstation through a tunnel, with frozen item IDs and paired analysis. At n=100 a 70 % score carries a ±9-point 95 % interval ([Miller, arXiv 2411.00640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640)) (C82). Chat-template and tool-call parsing failures look like quality loss, so `tool_bench.py` belongs in every model's first run. Pruned checkpoints need a non-English prompt set: a REAP-pruned card warns that other languages will degrade "likely severely" ([hellohazime card](https://huggingface.co/hellohazime/Kimi-K3-REAP640-IQ1_S-GGUF)).

## A five-phase backlog runs from calibration to long-shot engines

The backlog merges and de-duplicates the experiment ideas from all eight notes into 81 experiments. **Phase 0** builds the infrastructure and calibrates one card at a time with tiny models. **Phase 1** establishes the first MoE baselines on mainline llama.cpp. **Phase 2** tunes, patches and tries forks. **Phase 3** takes on stretch models and the workstation as a partner. **Phase 4** probes alternative engines. Each phase assumes the earlier phases' infrastructure, and explicit dependencies are listed per experiment so the list can be worked through one by one.

### The standard operating loop every experiment reuses

**Keep on the workstation.** The workstation keeps the model library (`/models/<repo>/<file>.gguf`), a manifest with each file's size and Hugging Face LFS sha256 (hashed once, at download, with `hf download` and `hf_xet` installed), the build factory, KLD reference logits and the evaluation harnesses. With a few hundred GB of free disk and tens of GB of RAM, it can hold dozens of quants plus a Qwen3.5-122B BF16 (~244 GB) if needed.

**Copy to the rig one at a time.** Delete the previous model first. Push with `rsync -a --partial --inplace --info=progress2 -e "ssh -c aes128-gcm@openssh.com -o Compression=no"` (AES-NI is present; GGUFs are incompressible), and keep at least ~2 GB free. That costs ~2 minutes per 12 GB and ~7.5 minutes per 50 GB at gigabit line rate ([ops note §1](notes/ops_and_benchmarking.md)). Rely on rsync's transfer checksum rather than re-hashing on the Celeron.

**Or mount instead of copying.** A read-only NFS export (`ro,vers=4.2,rsize=1048576,noatime`, raised read-ahead) avoids rig disk entirely. If claim C26 holds, loading with `-lm none` lets the mount drop after load. Installing the NFS client on the rig is a package change, flagged **[GO-AHEAD]** below.

**Canary residency trade-off.** Keeping a dense canary (Llama-2-7B Q4_0, ~3.8 GB) and a small-MoE canary (~4–5 GB) permanently on the rig cuts the local budget from ~24 GB to ~16 GB. That no longer fits Qwen3.6 UD-Q4_K_XL (22.36 GB) or Nemotron Bolt "24GB" (22.02 GB). The alternatives are keeping canaries on the workstation and copying them in ~40 s each, or loading the bigger models over NFS.

**Builds.** Builds are tens-of-MB tarballs from the workstation container, unpacked into versioned directories (`<engine>-<date>-<shortsha>-<flagtag>/` with a `BUILDINFO.json`). They link dynamically against the rig's existing CUDA 12.9 libraries. The tested rig's original on-rig build (llama.cpp commit 85d5703a3, 2026-09-04) is the reference that workstation builds are compared against.

**Serving.** `llama-bench` needs no network listener. For API-level tests, bind `llama-server` to localhost and reach it through an SSH local forward from the workstation, so no new LAN listener opens on the rig.

**Logging and cleanup.** Every run writes rows to the results log described in the baseline-protocol section, with raw `nvidia-smi` CSVs referenced by path. Delete the model after the experiment unless the next experiment reuses it.

**Legend.** Effort: Low ≤1 h, Medium ≤½ day, High 1–3 days, Very high a week or more. **[GO-AHEAD]** marks experiments that change the rig: package installs, network listeners, mounts, display-manager, power, clock or boot changes. Unlike read-only inspection, these alter the machine's state, so each deserves a deliberate decision before it runs. **[KEYBOARD]** marks experiments that may need a reboot or power cycle, which on an install with passphrase-only disk encryption, like the tested one, means someone at the rig to type the LUKS passphrase.

**Dependency spine.** E0.1 and E0.7 come before everything. The build chain is E0.3 → E0.4 → all workstation-built engines. The storage chain is E0.5 → E0.6 → every model larger than ~24 GB. Calibration runs E0.8 → E0.9 → E1.1 (the first MoE baseline) → the rest of Phase 1. E1.2 (a current build) gates E1.8, E1.12 and E3.1, the models that need code newer than 2026-09-04. The ik chain is E2.11 → E2.12 → E3.8. The RPC chain is E3.14 → E3.15 → E3.16 → E3.17–E3.20. The Python chain is E4.1 → E4.2–E4.5, and the ninfer chain is E4.10 → E4.11 → E4.12.

**E0.1 · Read-only rig inventory.** *Purpose:* freeze the starting state and answer C4, C20, C21 and C27 without changing anything. *Depends on:* nothing. *Setup:* one script run over SSH collects `nvidia-smi -q -d PERFORMANCE,CLOCK,SUPPORTED_CLOCKS,POWER,PCIE`, per-card `memory.used` at idle (the Xorg footprint), the exact `libcudart`/`libcublas` versions, the existing build's `--version` and system-info line (ARCHS, USE_GRAPHS) plus a verbose start that shows whether graphs are reported disabled, `lspci -vvv` link and ASPM state for the GPUs and switch ports, the ASPM policy, CPU flags, `cryptsetup benchmark`, `free -m`, `df -h` and swap. *Measure:* everything listed; memory clock and P-state *during* CUDA load are read later, in E0.8. *Expect:* graphs disabled (C4), no locked-clock support (C20), AES-XTS decrypt well above the SSD's ~485 MB/s (C27). *Effort/risk:* Low / none. *Disk:* none. *Easiest way:* keep the script under version control and store each dated output in a results directory.

**E0.2 · Network baseline.** *Purpose:* replace guessed network numbers with measured ones for NFS, rsync and RPC (C74). *Depends on:* nothing. *Setup:* `ping` over the plain LAN address and, if one is used, over a WireGuard-based mesh VPN in both directions, then `iperf3` with single and parallel streams both ways while recording rig CPU with `mpstat`. *Measure:* RTT p50/p99, Mbit/s, Celeron CPU %. *Expect:* ~940 Mbit/s and 0.2–0.4 ms on the LAN; ~2 ms over the VPN, possibly with lower throughput and high CPU. *Effort/risk:* Low / low. **[GO-AHEAD]** if iperf3 has to be installed or run as a server on the rig. Running the server on the workstation and a copied static client on the rig avoids both. *Disk:* none.

**E0.3 · Workstation build factory.** *Purpose:* reproducible sm_61, no-AVX builds without touching the rig (C30, C33). *Depends on:* nothing. *Setup:* the `nvidia/cuda:12.9.2-devel-ubuntu24.04` image pinned by digest, a ccache volume, and mainline at the rig's commit 85d5703a3 plus current master. Configure with `-DGGML_CUDA=ON -DCMAKE_CUDA_ARCHITECTURES=61 -DGGML_NATIVE=OFF -DGGML_AVX=OFF -DGGML_AVX2=OFF -DGGML_FMA=OFF -DGGML_F16C=OFF -DGGML_BMI2=OFF -DGGML_AVX512=OFF -DGGML_SSE42=ON -DLLAMA_CURL=OFF` with an `$ORIGIN/../lib` RPATH. Variants for later phases: `GGML_RPC=ON`, `GGML_CUDA_FA_QUANTS=all`, `GGML_CUDA_NCCL=OFF`, and the PR #27721 graphs patch. *Measure:* build success, `objdump -d libggml-cpu.so | grep -c ymm` = 0, highest GLIBC symbol ≤2.39, tarball size. *Expect:* clean builds of tens of MB. A native build on a workstation with glibc ≥2.41 (such as Ubuntu 26.04) is expected to hit the glibc header conflict, an optional check of C33. *Effort/risk:* Medium the first time / none to the rig. *Disk:* ~6 GB image on the workstation. *Easiest way:* a script under version control that emits `engine-date-sha-flags.tar.zst`.

**E0.4 · Binary parity.** *Purpose:* prove that workstation builds behave like the rig's own build (C31). *Depends on:* E0.3. *Setup:* unpack the tarball for the rig's commit into a versioned directory, check with `ldd` that CUDA 12.9 libraries resolve, then run the same greedy, fixed-seed prompt and the same `llama-bench -p 512 -n 128 -d 0,8192 -r 5` on one card with both binaries and Llama-2-7B Q4_0. *Measure:* token identity, tok/s difference against 2σ, `ARCHS = 610` in the log, no PTX JIT. *Expect:* identical tokens and speed. *Effort/risk:* Low / low. *Disk:* ~3.8 GB, kept as the dense canary.

**E0.5 · Model library and transfer methods.** *Purpose:* pick the copy path (C28). *Depends on:* E0.2. *Setup:* build the workstation library and manifest, then time one 10 GB GGUF through rsync over SSH with the default cipher, `aes128-gcm@openssh.com` and `chacha20-poly1305@openssh.com` (all with `Compression=no`), rsync daemon mode (the listener on the workstation), and an NFS `cp`. *Measure:* MB/s and rig CPU for each. *Expect:* ~110 MB/s with AES-GCM; the default cipher possibly lower. *Effort/risk:* Low / low. **[GO-AHEAD]** for the NFS client package if it is missing. *Disk:* 10 GB temporary, deleted afterwards.

**E0.6 · Load path: storage source × load mode.** *Purpose:* decide where models live and how they load (C23–C27, C29). *Depends on:* E0.4, E0.5. *Setup:* Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 Q4_K_M (18.56 GB) from local SSD, read-only NFS and a USB 3.0 SSD if one is available (`lsusb -t` must show `uas`), each with `-lm mmap`, `-lm none` and `-lm dio`. Run cold (drop caches), 3 repeats, timing process start to the first 200 on `/health`. *Measure:* wall-clock load time, peak RSS (`VmHWM`), per-card PCIe receive rate via `nvidia-smi dmon -s t`, NFS traffic during generation, and whether the share can be unmounted after a `-lm none` load. *Expect:* ~100 s locally, ~1.7× that over NFS, `dio` pathological on NFS, and mmap needing the share to stay mounted. *Effort/risk:* Medium / low. **[GO-AHEAD]** for the temporary NFS mount (and a USB SSD, if used). *Disk:* 18.6 GB locally; keep the file for E1.1.

**E0.7 · Results log, manifest and canary set.** *Purpose:* one schema and one canary set so every later number is comparable. *Depends on:* nothing. *Setup:* create the SQLite/JSONL log described in the baseline-protocol section, write the host-state capture script, freeze the canary GGUFs by hash and decide whether they live on the rig or the workstation (see the residency trade-off above). *Effort/risk:* Low / none.

**E0.8 · Single-card calibration against the P106-100 scoreboard.** *Purpose:* answer the 5-SM question that shifts every estimate by ±30–50 % (C1, C2, C3), and find weak cards or risers. *Depends on:* E0.4, or the rig build alone. *Setup:* `llama-bench -m llama-2-7b.Q4_0.gguf -ngl 99 -sm none -mg N -fa 0,1 -p 512 -n 128 -r 5` for N = 0…8, then Q4_K_M, IQ4_XS and Q8_0 of the same model on one card. Log `pstate`, `clocks.mem` and `clocks.sm` during the run (C20) and check `dmesg` for Xid errors, which mark marginal risers ([#20052](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/20052)). *Measure:* tg and pp per card, effective GB/s (file bytes × tg), tok/s per GB across quant formats. *Expect:* tg of either 26–31 tok/s (5 SMs suffice) or 16–22 (MMVQ-limited); pp ~190–240. *Effort/risk:* Low / none. *Disk:* ~4–7 GB per quant, one at a time. *Easiest way:* keep Q4_0 as the dense canary and delete the others afterwards.

**E0.9 · Launch-overhead probes with tiny MoEs.** *Purpose:* find out whether decode is CPU-bound (C6, C35). *Depends on:* E0.8. *Setup:* on one card, run the exact models from PR #27721 (OLMoE-1B-7B Q4_K_M 4.21 GB, Ling-3.0-tiny 4.92 GB at Q4_K_M or its Q6_K/Q8_0, LFM2.5-8B-A1B Q4_K_M 5.32 GB, granite-3.1-3b-a800m Q4_K_M) with `llama-bench -n 64,128 -d 0,4096`. Repeat with `-t 1` and `-t 2`, and with a busy-loop pinned to one core versus none, while watching `nvidia-smi dmon -s u`. *Measure:* tg against the 150–390 tok/s ceilings and against the GTX 1080 (52–150) and P102-100 (70.8) numbers in [PR #27721](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/27721); GPU utilisation; sensitivity to CPU contention. *Expect:* tg far below the ceilings and clearly sensitive to CPU contention, meaning launch-bound. *Effort/risk:* Low / none. *Disk:* ~4–5 GB each, one at a time; one of them can become the small-MoE canary.

**E0.10 · Code-path audit.** *Purpose:* confirm nothing silently runs on the Celeron and that graphs really are off (C4). *Depends on:* E0.9; it can also ride along with E1.1, E1.4 and E1.11. *Setup:* `GGML_SCHED_DEBUG=1` or verbose logging with a Qwen3 MoE, a Qwen3.6 GDN model and gpt-oss-20b across `-ctk/-ctv` f16, q8_0 and q4_0, with flash attention on and off. *Measure:* which backend each node lands on, and any "graphs disabled" message. *Expect:* everything on GPU for head sizes 64/128/256 with f16 or q8_0 KV; graphs disabled. *Effort/risk:* Low / none.

**E0.11 · PCIe, host-staging, EP and NCCL microbenchmarks.** *Purpose:* replace 2013-era latency estimates with measurements for T_hop, all-reduce and EP broadcast and gather (C7, C11, C12). *Depends on:* nothing. *Setup:* a small CUDA program built with `/usr/local/cuda-12.9/bin/nvcc -arch=sm_61` times `cudaMemcpyPeerAsync` without peer access and explicit pinned D2H+H2D at 4 KB, 8 KB, 16 KB, 608 KB and 4 MB. It covers same-switch (PIX), cross-switch (PHB) and GPU4 pairs with 1, 4 and 9 concurrent pairs, plus an "8 KB to k GPUs, gather k × 8 KB" EP pattern for k = 2…8. Add `nccl-tests` `all_reduce_perf` and `all_gather_perf` from 4 B to 1 MB across 2/4/8/9 GPUs, if the NCCL runtime the rig's NCCL=ON build links against is present. *Measure:* µs latency, bandwidth, and whether a staged copy blocks other streams. *Expect:* 50–200 µs at 8 KB, ~3 ms at 608 KB, visible contention behind each switch; the EP sync costing more than it saves for 3B-active models. *Effort/risk:* Medium / none. *Disk:* negligible.

**E0.12 · Thermal steady state and power-log validity.** *Purpose:* know when a run is thermally valid and how to compute tokens per joule. *Depends on:* E0.9 or E1.1. *Setup:* a 30-minute continuous decode with `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=timestamp,index,power.draw,clocks.sm,clocks.mem,temperature.gpu,pstate,clocks_event_reasons.active --format=csv -lms 200`, compared against 1 s sampling and optionally a wall-plug meter. Pascal reports only instantaneous power samples ([nvidia-smi docs](https://docs.nvidia.com/deploy/nvidia-smi/index.html)), and field names must be checked with `--help-query-gpu` on driver 580. *Measure:* per-card temperature, clock and throttle reasons over time; tok/s at minute 1 vs minute 30; inner vs outer slots. *Expect:* stable below 83 °C, with inner cards warmer. *Effort/risk:* Low / none.

Then run `llama-cli --rpc <rig>:50052 --list-devices`, and verify from a third host that the port is unreachable. Set `GGML_CUDA_DISABLE_GRAPHS=1` on the server if the RPC graph leak applies ([#20315](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/issues/20315)). *Measure:* device list, reported free VRAM, protocol handshake, tunnel throughput (the Celeron's encryption cost). *Expect:* nine RPC devices visible. *Effort/risk:* Low–medium / medium: this is a remote-code-execution surface with 2026 CVE-class fixes ([PR #20908](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/20908)). **[GO-AHEAD].**

**E3.15 · RPC load path and rig memory.** *Purpose:* confirm that RPC removes the rig's disk and page-cache limits (C73, C79). *Depends on:* E3.14. *Setup:* Qwen3-30B-A3B Q4_K_M over RPC with `-ngl 99` and no `-c`, then a ~45 GB model. Optionally use `-c` with `LLAMA_CACHE` on a USB SSD, since the internal disk cannot hold a 45 GB cache. *Measure:* load time, NIC throughput, rig `VmHWM`, PCIe rate, cache size. *Expect:* rig RSS near the largest tensor; 6.4–10.4 min for a cold 45 GB load. *Effort/risk:* Medium / low.

**E3.16 · Standalone versus RPC on the same model.** *Purpose:* price the network tax (C75). *Depends on:* E3.15, E1.1. *Setup:* Qwen3-30B-A3B Q4_K_M in three arms: (A) rig standalone `llama-bench`; (B) workstation client with RPC0–8 only, output layer on the rig; (C) output layer on the workstation (its GPU or CPU) via device order or `-ot`. Add `GGML_RPC_DEBUG=3 -lv 5` to count commands and bytes per token. *Measure:* tok/s, ms per token, bytes per token, rig CPU %. *Expect:* B slower than A by a few ms per token, C in between, and lower rig CPU in B and C because tokenization and sampling move to the workstation CPU. *Effort/risk:* Medium / low.

**E3.17 · One rpc-server versus nine.** *Purpose:* confirm the topology (C76). *Depends on:* E3.16. *Setup:* nine `rpc-server` instances (`--device CUDAi`, ports 50052–50060) versus one, repeating arm B. *Measure:* tok/s. *Expect:* nine servers slower, because each boundary bounces through the client. *Effort/risk:* Low / low. **[GO-AHEAD]** (nine listeners).

**E3.18 · Workstation GPU + rig whole-layer split.** *Purpose:* ~62 GB of combined VRAM, with an 8 GB workstation card, for models just over the rig's limit (C78). *Depends on:* E3.16. *Setup:* `-dev RPC0,…,RPC8,CUDA0 -ts …` with the final layers and output head on the workstation GPU, first on a model that fits 54 GB (apples to apples), then on one that needs more (for example Nemotron Bolt UD-Q4_K_XL at 50.22 GB, or a larger-context GLM-Air). *Measure:* tok/s, VRAM per device. *Expect:* a small tax and a real capacity gain. *Effort/risk:* Medium / low.

**E3.19 · Attention on the workstation GPU, experts on the rig.** *Purpose:* validate the network cost model, with a negative result expected (C77). *Depends on:* E3.16. *Setup:* non-expert tensors on CUDA0 (the workstation GPU) and `-ot "ffn_.*_exps=RPC[...]"` spread across the rig's devices. *Measure:* tg, pp, NIC utilisation. *Expect:* tg far below standalone and prefill capped near ~150 tok/s; a much better result would mean the cost model needs revising. *Effort/risk:* Low / low.

**E3.20 · Speculative decoding over RPC.** *Purpose:* test whether speculation amortises round trips (C61). *Depends on:* E3.16, E2.6. *Setup:* Qwen3.6 MTP (or a draft model on the workstation GPU) in topology B/C against standalone. *Measure:* acceptance and tg ratios. *Expect:* a larger relative gain over RPC. *Effort/risk:* Medium / low.

## One canary protocol and one log schema make every run comparable

Every model swap costs minutes, so comparability has to come from discipline rather than repetition: fixed files pinned by hash, fixed commands, recorded host state, and a canary rerun after every change to the engine, build, driver, host or hardware. llama-bench already records its build commit, model file and every runtime setting in JSON, JSONL or SQL output ([llama-bench README](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/llama-bench/README.md)), and `compare-llama-bench.py` diffs two commits from its SQLite output ([compare-llama-bench.py](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/scripts/compare-llama-bench.py)). The proposal below wraps those tools with what they do not know: storage source, host state, power, thermals and quality. It is a proposal, built from the ops note's options, to adapt to each setup.

### A proposed three-tier baseline protocol

| Tier | When to run it | Contents | Rough time | Acceptance rule (proposal) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A: minimal canary** | After every engine, build, flag, driver or host change | Host-state capture → 3-min warm-up decode (discarded) → fixed `llama-bench` on a fixed 1–3-card subset with the small-MoE canary → `tool_bench.py run --n 10 --temp 0` → KLD on 25 chunks against the stored reference | ~20–30 min including load | pp/tg within the canary's rolling mean ± 2σ; KLD unchanged within its printed ±; tool-call success unchanged |
| **B: full-rig canary** | After any change touching multi-GPU, storage or loading, and weekly | Tier A plus the full-rig canary: load time from the chosen storage source (process start → `/health` 200), pp/tg at depths 0 and 8192, TTFT at 512 and 4k prompts via the API client (`cache_prompt=false`), and a power log turned into tokens per joule | ~1 h | Same rule on every metric, plus load time within ±10 % |
| **C: per-new-model** | Once for every model or quant that reaches the shortlist | Tier B's speed suite on the new model + KLD on 50–100 chunks against a workstation Q8_0/BF16 reference + `tool_bench.py` + paired subsets with frozen item IDs (IFEval 150, GSM8K 250, HumanEval+ 164) + the E1.15 wall-clock prompt set; an Aider polyglot slice only for finalists | Several hours | Comparison by paired differences against the current best model, never against memory |

| Canary role | Options | Why | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dense calibration | Llama-2-7B Q4_0 (~3.8 GB) | Directly comparable with the CUDA and Vulkan scoreboards ([#15013](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/15013), [#10879](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/discussions/10879)) | Not a MoE |
| Small MoE, 1–3 cards | OLMoE-1B-7B Q4_K_M (4.21 GB), Ling-3.0-tiny Q4_K_M (4.92 GB), or gpt-oss-20b MXFP4 (12.11 GB, 3 cards) | OLMoE Q4_K_M matches PR #27721's GTX 1080 run, and Ling-3.0-tiny at Q6_K/Q8_0 its GTX 1080 and P102-100 runs; gpt-oss exercises multi-GPU hops | Tiny models are launch-bound, so they exaggerate CPU effects; gpt-oss costs disk |
| Full rig | Qwen3-30B-A3B Q4_K_M (18.56 GB) or Qwen3.6-35B-A3B UD-Q4_K_XL (22.36 GB) | The mature control, or the likely daily driver | Either one, plus the canaries above, exceeds the local disk; one of them lives on NFS |

The common rules are fixed: the same canary files (hash-pinned) forever; the same prompts and item IDs; host state recorded for every run; one variable changed at a time; interleaved A/B runs to cancel thermal and background drift; at least 5 repetitions; differences inside ~2σ treated as noise; per-card temperatures logged; and results written to the log before anyone interprets them. Thinking models get a stated `max_tokens` cap, or thinking disabled where supported, and the cap is recorded.

```bash
# Fixed speed command (mainline; ik_llama.cpp and other forks need their own equivalents)
llama-bench -m "$CANARY" -ngl 99 -fa on -p 512 -n 128 -d 0,4096 -r 5 --delay 5 -o jsonl > "$RUN_DIR/bench.jsonl"

# Power and thermal log alongside every run (check field names with --help-query-gpu on driver 580)
nvidia-smi --query-gpu=timestamp,index,power.draw,clocks.sm,clocks.mem,temperature.gpu,utilization.gpu,memory.used,pstate,clocks_event_reasons.active \
  --format=csv,noheader,nounits -lms 200 > "$RUN_DIR/gpu.csv" &

# KLD against a stored reference (identical -c and --chunks on both sides)
llama-perplexity -m "$MODEL" -f "$TEXT" -c 512 --chunks 25 --kl-divergence-base "$REF.kld" --kl-divergence > "$RUN_DIR/kld.txt"
```

### A results-log schema to reuse after every change

The schema mirrors the ops note's proposal. It stores llama-bench rows verbatim, so the upstream comparison scripts keep working, and adds the fields llama-bench cannot know. It can live as `results.sqlite` under version control, with an append-only JSONL mirror per campaign for diffs. Raw `nvidia-smi` CSVs and server logs stay outside git, referenced by path and hash.

```sql
CREATE TABLE runs (
  run_id TEXT PRIMARY KEY,            -- e.g. 2026-10-23T14:05Z-E1.4-a
  experiment_id TEXT NOT NULL,        -- E1.4
  claim_ids TEXT,                     -- "C34,C37"
  timestamp_utc TEXT NOT NULL, operator TEXT, notes TEXT,
  -- engine and build
  engine TEXT NOT NULL,               -- llama.cpp | ik_llama.cpp | fastllm | ...
  engine_commit TEXT NOT NULL, engine_build_number TEXT,
  build_host TEXT,                    -- rig | workstation-docker
  container_digest TEXT, cuda_version TEXT, cmake_flags TEXT,
  patches TEXT,                       -- "pr27721,issue30334"
  binary_path TEXT,
  -- host state
  driver_version TEXT, kernel TEXT,
  headless INTEGER, persistence_mode INTEGER,
  ram_total_gib REAL, free_ram_before_mib INTEGER, page_cache_dropped INTEGER,
  power_limit_w TEXT,                 -- JSON array of 9
  clock_offsets TEXT,                 -- JSON, empty when stock
  aspm_policy TEXT,
  -- model and storage
  model_repo TEXT, model_file TEXT, quant TEXT,
  file_size_bytes INTEGER, file_sha256 TEXT, imatrix_source TEXT,
  n_params_total REAL, n_params_active REAL,
  storage_source TEXT,                -- local-ssd | nfs | usb-ssd | rpc-stream
  load_mode TEXT,                     -- mmap | none | dio
  -- placement and runtime flags
  gpu_set TEXT, split_mode TEXT, tensor_split TEXT, override_tensor TEXT,
  ctx_size INTEGER, type_k TEXT, type_v TEXT, flash_attn TEXT,
  n_batch INTEGER, n_ubatch INTEGER, n_parallel INTEGER,
  spec_type TEXT, spec_draft_n_max INTEGER,
  expert_used_count INTEGER,          -- NULL = model default
  rpc_topology TEXT                   -- NULL | T1 | T3 | c1 | ...
);

CREATE TABLE speed (                  -- llama-bench rows map 1:1; API client adds ttft/e2e
  run_id TEXT REFERENCES runs(run_id),
  test TEXT,                          -- pp | tg | pg | ttft | e2e | load | batched
  n_prompt INTEGER, n_gen INTEGER, n_depth INTEGER, n_parallel INTEGER,
  value REAL,                         -- tok/s; ms for ttft; s for load
  stddev REAL, repetitions INTEGER,
  samples TEXT,                       -- JSON array (llama-bench samples_ts)
  acceptance REAL                     -- speculative runs only
);

CREATE TABLE resources (
  run_id TEXT REFERENCES runs(run_id),
  gpu_index INTEGER,                  -- 0..8; NULL = host
  vram_used_mib INTEGER, temp_max_c REAL,
  sm_clock_mhz_median REAL, mem_clock_mhz_median REAL, pstate TEXT,
  throttle_events TEXT, energy_j REAL, host_rss_peak_mib INTEGER
);

CREATE TABLE quality (
  run_id TEXT REFERENCES runs(run_id),
  metric TEXT,                        -- ppl | kld_mean | kld_p99 | same_top_p | task:<name>
  value REAL, ci95 REAL,
  dataset TEXT, dataset_version TEXT,
  item_ids_hash TEXT, n_items INTEGER,
  ctx INTEGER, chunks INTEGER,
  kld_reference TEXT,                 -- file + quant + engine that produced the base logits
  harness_version TEXT, max_tokens INTEGER, thinking INTEGER
);

CREATE TABLE artifacts (
  run_id TEXT REFERENCES runs(run_id),
  kind TEXT,                          -- bench-jsonl | gpu-csv | server-log | kld | inventory
  path TEXT, sha256 TEXT
);
```

No multi-GPU MoE result exists on Pascal mining cards, on PCIe x1 risers, or with six or more cards. No one has published CUDA host-staged small-copy latency on PCIe 1.0 x1, `-sm tensor` or ik `-sm graph` over x1 without P2P, or batch-1 expert parallelism anywhere: ThunderEP starts at batch 6 and has no public code. Per-node kernel-launch cost on a low-end mobile Celeron is unmeasured, and so is CPU sampling cost over 150–250k vocabularies without SIMD. There are no Pascal measurements for gpt-oss MXFP4 with sinks, Gemma 4's head-512 attention in mainline, Nemotron-H's SSM scan, LFM2 convolutions, Kimi-Linear's KDA, or Nemotron Bolt's heterogeneous experts. Nobody has documented whether ik_llama.cpp runs on a CPU without AVX. Load times from NFS, SMB or NBD on a 4 GB-RAM host with a fully offloaded multi-GPU model are unpublished, as is whether `--no-mmap` lets a share unmount. No same-memory head-to-head of a big MoE at ~3 bpw against a small MoE at Q8, or of a REAP checkpoint against an unpruned quant of equal size, exists for the 2026 models. The Qwen3.5-122B REAP-20/30, Qwen3-Next-REAP, MiniMax-REAP and gpt-oss-REAP-58B checkpoints have no published benchmarks at all. Vulkan MoE numbers on Pascal, the real compute-buffer sizes per card, and how llama.cpp places Qwen3.8's 51B-parameter n-gram table are all open. Each of these gaps is a claim in the table above and an experiment in the backlog, and results from this rig would be among the first published for this hardware class.

## Conclusion

The decisive unknowns are properties of the platform, not of any model or engine. Two cheap Phase 0 measurements will re-price every estimate in this report: single-card decode against the P106-100 scoreboard (E0.8) and the CPU sensitivity of tiny-MoE decode (E0.9). Whether five SMs can stream ~170 GB/s, and whether a 1.8 GHz Celeron can launch ~2,000 kernels per token fast enough, matters more than the choice between Qwen3.6, Nemotron and Gemma 4, whose bandwidth ceilings all fall in the same 52–94 tok/s band. If the launch-bound hypothesis holds, the most valuable change on the whole backlog may be a one-line gate edit that turns CUDA graphs back on for sm_61 (E2.1). That would beat any quant, fork or engine swap, and it costs one rebuild.

The rig's other surprise is that its storage problem is milder than it looks. llama.cpp's loader already serialises uploads onto one x1 link at ~190 MB/s, so a gigabit NFS mount costs well under twice the local load time. That turns "one model at a time on a 26 GB disk" from a hard limit into a workflow choice. It also makes the rig a natural lab for two questions the community has not answered with measurements: whether a 100B-class MoE at ~3 bpw beats a 35B MoE at Q8 in the same memory, and whether pruning beats quantisation at equal size. Answering them on nine retired mining cards would be more informative than any single tok/s figure.
