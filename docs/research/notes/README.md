# Research notes: MoE inference on the P106 rig

These are the underlying research notes that the report [Fitting 2026's MoE models onto nine P106s](../moe-inference-on-the-p106-rig.md) synthesises, one note per topic, compiled as of 2026-10-22. They hold the full source lists, per-quant file sizes, tables and arithmetic that the report summarises, along with each topic's own claims to test and experiment ideas. Nothing in them was measured on the rig unless stated; measured results are in [docs/benchmarks/](../../benchmarks/), and the hardware is described in the [hardware spec](../../mining_rig.md).

- [models_small_moe.md](models_small_moe.md): catalogue of small and mid-size MoE models (up to ~40B total), with GGUF sizes, disk and VRAM fit, KV-cache cost and decode-speed ceilings.
- [models_large_moe_and_compression.md](models_large_moe_and_compression.md): 40–150B+ "stretch" MoE models, per-layer packing into nine 6 GB cards, and MoE compression (low-bit quants, expert pruning and merging, expert-count overrides).
- [multigpu_pascal_performance.md](multigpu_pascal_performance.md): where decode and prefill time goes on Pascal over PCIe 1.0 x1 without P2P: bandwidth efficiency, kernel-launch overhead, split modes and tuning knobs.
- [engines_llamacpp_family.md](engines_llamacpp_family.md): mainline llama.cpp on sm_61, ik_llama.cpp, Pascal patch forks, the Vulkan and RPC backends, and wrappers.
- [engines_other.md](engines_other.md): every inference engine outside the llama.cpp family (vLLM and its Pascal forks, SGLang, ExLlama, MLC-LLM, Strata, Maya and others), checked against the rig.
- [engine_infer.md](engine_infer.md): ninfer (the engine referred to as "infer"), the similarly named DeepInfer, and their feasibility on sm_61.
- [distributed_inference.md](distributed_inference.md): splitting a model across machines, mainly llama.cpp RPC between the rig and a more powerful PC on the same LAN, with a network cost model.
- [ops_and_benchmarking.md](ops_and_benchmarking.md): storing, moving and loading models on a small disk, building sm_61 engines off the rig, and methods for measuring speed and quality.
- [dense_27b_speedups.md](dense_27b_speedups.md): techniques for faster generation of the dense Qwen3.8-27B: speculative decoding (MTP, DFlash, DSpark, draft models, n-gram), tensor parallelism over x1 links, quant formats for 5-SM cards, activation sparsity, runtime levers and pruned variants, with ranked experiments.
- [flint_speedups.md](flint_speedups.md): techniques for faster generation of Recursive's Flint (40B-A2.5B MoE): its from-scratch lineage and what that rules out, n-gram speculation, llama.cpp's MoE activation rescale and how to remove it safely, expert-count reduction, Pascal kernel levers, per-token byte layouts for quantization, slots and serving settings, other engines, with ranked experiments.
- [tqllm.md](tqllm.md): tqLLM (the engine referred to as "tqllm") and the TurboQuant formats behind it: what it is, how it compares with Strata, why the engine cannot run on sm_61, why its TQ3_4S weights are likely slower than Q4_0 on 5-SM cards, which TurboQuant idea (KV-cache rotation, already in mainline llama.cpp) is usable today, with ranked experiments.
- Quant-speed research (2026-10-22/23), summarized in [quants/research.md](../../../quants/research.md): what can be done to a model file, its formats or by retraining to make it decode faster on 5-SM Pascal cards. [Starting facts](quant_speed_brief.md); [file-only levers](quant_speed_a_file_only.md) (mixes, the rescale, rounding, rotations, fused tensors, head, pruning; a decode-time model fitted on Flint); [formats and kernels](quant_speed_b_formats_kernels.md) (why the cards are instruction-bound, SASS counts per format, Pascal kernel levers, group-64, FP4); [retraining](quant_speed_c_retraining.md) (EAGLE-3 and block drafters, quantization-aware recovery, pruning with retraining, costs on a 7-GPU machine); [community reports](quant_speed_d_community.md) (claims log from Pascal owners: Vulkan, flags, Flint and Qwen3.6 reports).

- Pascal engine work (2026-10-23): [porting the Pascal matrix-vector kernels into llama.cpp](pascal_mmvq_integration.md) (design for Q38/Q39: hook, types in order, fusions, repacking, tests, expected 34–36 tok/s for S4-L0, an ~8-day plan, four upstream PRs); [K-quant decode on the P106-090](kquant_decode_pascal.md) (why Unsloth's UD-Q4_K_XL decodes slowly, what Pascal K/I-quant kernels would cost, and an estimated 31–33 tok/s for it, the same as the simple-format route); [upstream status of the Pascal changes](upstream_pascal_patches.md) (each related PR and issue, what a PR must show, conflicts with master, draft PR texts).

- HyperQwen (2026-10-23): [what HyperQwen does to Qwen3.8-27B and what carries over to Flint](hyperqwen_lessons.md) (speculative decoding on a dense model with a nearly free verify step; why Flint's expert fan-out changes the arithmetic; copy-gated lookup drafting, CUDA graphs per verify length, draft-head economics, backend sampling for tool requests, prompt checkpoints, attention at depth; what does not transfer; ranked experiments Q51–Q56).

- Project Maya (2026-10-23): [its VRAM–RAM–NVMe expert tiering and quants, and what they mean for the rig](maya_expert_tiering.md) (how the three-tier expert cache and the GLM-5.3-Flash quants work; why tiering solves capacity, not speed; the rig's I/O path against one expert miss; GLM-5.3-Flash modelled at ~0.4–1 tok/s; why NVMe or more RAM would not change that; the two small ideas that carry over).

- Planned-work research (2026-10-23): [attention at long context](pascal_attention_long_context.md) (which flash-attention kernel compute 6.1 runs for Flint, why it reads the KV cache at 57–87 GB/s, why q8_0 KV is slower and saves little VRAM, a Pascal decode kernel that reads K/V once for all eight query heads, projected tok/s from 8k to 262k; Q54, Q59–Q63); [speculation plumbing](speculation_plumbing.md) (what a batch-shape change costs in llama-context and the CUDA graph cache, upstream PR #31004, the copy-gated `ngram-copy` drafter, a cost model per workload; Q52, Q53, Q64, Q65); [sampling for tool requests](tool_request_sampling.md) (why every tool request samples on the host, the 3.2–3.5 ms it costs per token, exact GPU-sampling designs, llguidance masks; Q55, Q66–Q69); [task-level quality checks](task_level_quality_checks.md) (run-to-run noise in the 300-task BFCL check, paired task counts per detectable difference, KLD as a filter rather than a ranking, tool-calling and coding benchmarks, the protocol for Q41 and the Q42 model cards; Q70–Q72).

- rec (2026-10-23): [Recursive's own engine for Flint and what carries over to the rig](rec_engine.md) (an Apple-only MLX/Metal engine that runs a whole token in one persistent kernel; Flint's exact numerics with BF16 storage and FP32 sums and no rescale; the group-64 INT4 export behind the makers' Q4_1; no speculative decoding and no MTP head; the makers' sampling, history and tool-call settings; a decode-attention layout for Q59/Q60; a GPU grammar mask for tool requests).

- Shared expert (2026-10-23): [Flint's shared-expert launches](shared_expert_launches.md) (why its gate/up products ran unfused on the CUDA backend and the fork's fix, Q50; a design for running it as a seventh expert inside the routed launches without changing any file's types, weighed against the GGUF-rewrite route, Q37).

- Long-context cliff (2026-10-23): [why S4-L0's decode fell from 23.3 tok/s at 32k to 3.6 at 64k in llama-bench](long_context_cliff.md) (most likely not attention: llama-bench keeps each depth's context state in host memory, 1.78 GiB at 64k, which pushes the 4 GB host into swap; the attention dispatch, mask, CUDA graphs and VRAM ruled out from the source; the rig experiments that confirm it; Q79).

- Breakthrough candidates (2026-10-23): [ideas for a large speed-up or quality gain that the registry has not covered](breakthrough_candidates.md) (pipelined speculation that sends guessed tokens through the idle cards as single-token steps, no batched verify, est. 1.2× overall with n-gram guesses and 1.5–2.4× on chat with a trained pipeline drafter; a persistent decode kernel per card; a 6-bit Q6_0 rung; an exact screened output head; KL-aware rounding; an attention mask computed on the card for long-context prefill; Quest-style KV page selection; intra-expert sparsity, expert layout and learned expert skipping; what was rejected by arithmetic and what it changes in the plan).
- Pipelined speculation (2026-10-24): [the executor in the fork and its correctness](pipelined_speculation.md) (how llama.cpp runs a layer split and why two tokens can't overlap today; PipeInfer, SPD, SpecPipe and FlowSpec; per-token slots with their own scheduler, graph and output event; wrong guesses dropped instead of withdrawn, free while D ≤ S_eff − 1; KV order, layout and sliding-window safety; 89 pipelined runs bit-identical to plain decoding; what llama-server would need; Q87).

- Unconventional ideas (2026-10-24): [skipping work, changing the model or format, and using the topology, priced against the quality bar](unconventional_ideas.md) (the bar leaves ~1 ms of lossy savings, so structural cuts were rejected; a first-finish race of 2–3 samples through the idle cards, est. 1.10–1.35× per answer; reflection-token suppression and concise self-training, est. 1.10–1.4×; KV-sharded decode attention, exact, ~1.5× at 131k and ~2× at 262k; guesses from a table of Flint's own output with Gumbel-coupled sampling for the pipelined executor; coarser scale groups, a Pascal router kernel and a split output head; what was rejected and why).

- Pascal forks and quant formats (2026-10-24): [who else optimized decode for Pascal and mining cards, and what a Pascal-native format would be](pascal_forks_and_quants.md) (llama.cpp forks for P100, P40 and GTX 10-series cards, other engines and CMP projects compared with the fork; the microbenchmark-versus-model gap explained by GPU page walks, ~1.3–2 µs per 2 MiB page, with translation prefetch and TLB-aware placement as fixes; norm-written Q8_1 activations, persistent layers and upstream PR #28874; Samsung's NanoQuant and LittleBit sub-1-bit methods rejected on quality and rank; K-quant kernels, a Pascal Q6_K rung first, instead of a new GGUF type; 3-bit experts fail the bar).

- Pipeline drafter (2026-10-24): [which one-token guesser should feed pipelined speculation and the first-finish race](pipeline_drafter.md) (why drafters that read the previous token's final hidden state, EAGLE-3, Medusa and MTP, allow only one or two guesses in flight; token-only and partial-depth guessers that never wait for the previous token; a partial-depth "staircase" head after SPD, 2 layers and a 32k head on the hidden states Flint already computes for the tokens in flight, Gumbel-coupled behind the copy lookup, est. p 0.60–0.72 at temperature 1.0 and 2.05–2.32× on six cards; placement on card 1 or a spare card; sharing the cards with the race; a 40–70 GPU-hour offline pilot scored with Q87's simulator).

- Multi-conversation serving (2026-10-24): [serving several conversations at once on the layer split](multi_conversation_serving.md) (llama-server's `-np N` batching against conversations staggered through the cards as one-token steps and groups of two on the multi-token kernels, est. 118–128 tok/s total at four conversations and 235–256 at eight on nine cards with each at 29–32 tok/s, against ~37–43 for `-np` today; KV per conversation and what fits beside a 25–33 GB file; Orca, DeepSpeed Inference, vLLM, SGLang, SpecPipe-DB and PipeInfer; the scheduler, llama-context and server changes with file:line pointers and a streams mode for the pipelined executor; one budget for conversations, guesses and race samples; rig experiments and a simulator extension).

- Long-context quality (2026-10-24): [why 512-token KLD may not predict long, multi-turn agent quality, and what to measure instead](long_context_quality.md) (on 512-token chunks Flint's 14 global layers see no more than its sliding-window layers, so their long-range role is never scored; UD-Q4_K_XL keeps attention at Q8_0 in 13 of 14 global layers, while G3 has Q5_0 q in seven and output in five; Q41's first multi-turn pair shows no trend with context length; transcript KLD at 8k on Flint's own BFCL conversations and wikitext KLD at 8k as the first cheap checks, est. 4–5 GPU-hours; all-global-attention Q8_0 "G3g" at +37.7 MB per token as the top fix if a gap shows; a decision procedure with sample sizes).

- Prompt processing (2026-10-24): [where a prompt token's time goes on nine P106 cards and how to make prefill several times faster](prefill_pascal.md) (below ~16k most of it is MMQ arithmetic on padding, because compute 6.1 tiles each expert for 64 tokens when it sees ~3, fixed by upstream PR #31047, est. 1.6–2.0× at 8k; llama.cpp's pipeline holds at most two micro-batches at `-ub 128` because reused graphs wait for all cards, est. 1.4–1.8× from a one-line fix or `-ub 256`; the attention mask's synchronous host copies, 18 B per prompt token per context token, and attention arithmetic dominate from ~32k; micro-batch × copies under 4 GB of host RAM; ~2 s fixed per request from backend-sampler re-reserves and checkpoints; caching already serves ~90 % of an agent session's prompt tokens and ~97.6 % with a saved system prompt, yet the fresh tool output keeps prefill the limit; prior art and rig experiments).

- Better rounding of the body (2026-10-24): [GPTQ, AWQ-style scaling and learned rounding for Flint's experts, attention and shared expert in Q4_1/Q5_0/Q6_K/Q8_0 blocks](gptq_body.md) (published gains against imatrix rounding are 0–13 % on dense models and 13 % on MoE experts, against 68 % on Flint's own head; routed experts gain bytes only through headroom, since Q5_0 → Q4_1 saves 9 % of their bytes and Q4_0 no time; the payoff is in Q77's expensive Q8_0 groups, whose inputs are the anisotropic ones; per-expert token counts and input statistics from Unsloth's importance matrix; exact AWQ folds in Flint, router included; est. +1.6–4.5 % tok/s at or under the bar; a ~3 GPU-hour first experiment against Q77's RTN files).

- The host in the decode loop (2026-10-24): [where the 2-core Celeron's time goes per token and how far it can leave the loop](host_decode_loop.md) (within a token the host stays ahead of the cards, between tokens a serial gap of est. 0.3–1.5 ms plus host-staged hops; llama-server ran ~8–10 ms per token slower than llama-bench before the fork, unexplained; CUDA's default yield-spin and libgomp's 300,000-spin worker keep both cores busy, so `-t 1`; streaming re-parses the whole reply per token; at eight conversations the ~50 synchronous input copies per token make the host the limit, fixed by one asynchronous packed upload per card plus the on-card decode mask; the sampled token fed to card 1 on the device, est. +2–5 %; conditional-node loops are single-device and blocking sync would hurt; a first rig measurement without CUDA events or Nsight).

- Answer time (2026-10-24): [does quantization change how long Flint reasons, and which release candidate gives the shortest time to a correct answer](answer_time.md) (paired by task on Q41's runs, G3 and K2h reason as long as UD-Q4_K_XL: E1 1.000 and 0.990, E2 0.957, E3 1.033, all inside the attempt-to-attempt noise of ±3 %, hesitation-word density level too; the literature's length inflation follows quantization damage, +3.7 % for GPTQ INT4 against +43 % for round-to-nearest on Qwen3-30B-A3B; a time-per-correct-answer model from bytes per token, prefill and pass rate puts K2h 2 % ahead of UD, a lead that 1.9–2.2 % of extra reasoning or one point of pass@1 would erase, and that the screened head on UD's Q8_0 head erases too; a TPCA release metric with a length gate; the `</think>` probability from Q106's dumps and E3's budget-decided problems at 64k as first experiments; [bench/answer_time](../../../bench/answer_time/)).

- Long-context mix (2026-10-24): [where a Flint file must spend its bytes to beat UD-Q4_K_XL at equal speed on long agent text](long_context_mix.md) (UD keeps attention at Q8_0 in 42 of 49 layers, we spend the bytes on experts; re-analysed, Q106's 8k gap is a tool-schema gap that grows with neither position nor copy distance, sits on heavy-tailed, bistable positions and on only 15 independent schema contexts, while on written tokens G3 is 0.89× UD; the GPTQ head costs +7 % and K2's late-layer groups +7 % of UD's written-token KLD at 8k, so the screened head and a 6-bit rung beat Q5_0 cuts; at UD's 2.06 GB: G3-6sr, est. KLD 0.105–0.106 and 0.90–0.91× UD on written tokens, needs Q101; K2sr today; swap probes, a cross-prefill test and a decision-point replay for miss_param as first experiments; a release rule with superiority on written tokens and non-inferiority elsewhere).

- Agent workload check (2026-10-24): [a realistic end-to-end check with the pi coding agent driving Flint on the rig](agent_workload_check.md) (what Terminal-Bench, SWE-bench, Aider polyglot, τ-bench and quant studies do and why their Docker-per-task harnesses do not fit the rig; pi's JSON mode, logs, ~1.4–1.8k-token prefix, reasoning replay, missing turn limit and costly compaction; a 48-task suite in six script-checked families, est. 8.1 h per pass on v1 and 5.5 h on v2; engine speed from per-request timings and trace replay, engine quality as a breakage gate, file quality on the quant machine at 48 × 10 per file; tool requests keep backend sampling and the screened head off; checkpoint eviction as the first pilot question; a ≤ 2 h pilot with go/no-go and a ~20 h rig day).

- Hardware tuning (2026-10-24): [free settings on the rig and how to prove each one safe](hardware_tuning.md) (a memory offset through NVML at runtime, no Xorg change, est. +3–5 % decode at +10 % memory clock because only ~55 % of a token is weight streaming, +5–9 % after Q88/Q99; GP106 margins of +500 to +1,000 MT/s reported by miners, Ethash a wrong proxy on 5 SMs; busy cards already at their top boost bin at ~35 W, so power limits, locked clocks and PowerMizer modes give nothing, a core offset est. +0.5–2 % decode and +3–5 % prefill; PCIe 2.0 impossible because the cards advertise 2.5 GT/s; ASPM already off; GDDR5's CRC covers only the data bus, so safety is a bit-exact one-token KLD against a stock base, a CUDA pattern test and a soak, one card and +200 MT/s at a time, with offset 0 as the rollback and a reboot only after a hang).

- Release v2 (2026-10-24): [how to ship v2 so an owner of the same rig gets its speed and quality in minutes, legally and reproducibly](release_v2.md) (stock llama.cpp refuses a GGUF with an extra tensor, "wrong number of tensors", so the screened head ships as a 173 MB side file that also serves UD-Q4_K_XL and the mixes stay standard GGUF; Flint is plain Apache-2.0, Unsloth's imatrix repository too, llama.cpp MIT, NVIDIA's runtime libraries redistributable; this repository has no licence and Unsloth-derived GGUF headers say "flint-preview" and "quantized_by Unsloth", both to fix; prebuilt tarballs plus a CUDA-runtime asset on the fork's GitHub Releases for glibc ≥ 2.38 and driver 580; command lines for chat, agents and long context; a one-page results format; a ranked checklist with the owner's decisions).

- v2 synthesis (2026-10-24): [what v2 delivers per workload on six and nine cards, and what ships when](v2_synthesis.md) (measured on nine cards with UD-Q4_K_XL: generation 2.2× stock and 1.12× v1 in one llama-bench session, served chat 31–34 tok/s, tool requests 29.3–29.6 because they sample on the host, prompts +22–29 % with PR #31047; the engine carries ≥ 95 % of the speed-up and the file 0–3 %; the combined build's decode loss was llama-bench state, not PR #31047; ~5 ms per token between llama-bench's best state and served speed is the largest open term; six cards as fast as nine at short context; per-token and per-turn budgets for chat, agents, long context, several conversations and prefill with each pending piece; v2.0 = the combined build with UD-Q4_K_XL, v2.1 = the screen, the decode mask, the multi-token kernels and the executor's pieces, later = GPU sampling for tool requests, the drafter, attention at depth; gaps with their cheapest measurements; a draft public summary).

- Power and tuning (2026-10-24): [what the cards draw, when the 75 W cap costs speed, and which documented settings help](power_and_tuning.md) (power follows duty, ~33 W waiting in P0 plus ~40 W × duty: 35–39 W per card in layer-split decode, ~70 W and capped at full duty, 6 W idle; every card busy est. 630–675 W of GPU power, ~800 W at the wall, half the 1,600 W supply, ~90–96 W per 6-pin input on average; the cap costs est. 2–4 % on decode stages and 6–9 % on prefill, so 10 % was conservative for decode; the larger loss is decode that starts cold, held at 1,354 MHz base clock by the utilization-based PowerMizer policy with no event bit, est. 14–18 % and most likely the v2 synthesis's ~5 ms state effect, fixed by "Prefer Maximum Performance" through NV-CONTROL if the X server exposes the cards; memory and core offsets under the cap; a 60 W limit for +10–20 % tokens per joule in all-busy modes; all-busy modes cut energy per token 1.8–3.6×; only idle, none and power-cap bits in 266k samples, the capping counter unusable; a 200 ms logging protocol, a wall-meter protocol and a ranked rig plan R0–R7).

- Fork scope (2026-10-24): [is the fork a Flint llama.cpp, a Pascal llama.cpp or a layer-split llama.cpp](fork_scope.md) (every change on `pascal-p106`, `pascal-p106-v2` and the feature branches classified as Pascal, MoE, generic or Flint against the code: 87 % of v2's decode gain on UD-Q4_K_XL is Pascal-only, the FP32 sums 12 %, nothing Flint-only; only the persistent MoE-layer kernel hard-codes Flint's shapes and router; which models (Qwen3.6, Qwen3-30B-A3B, gpt-oss, GLM-4.5-Air, DeepSeek-style, Ornith, dense) and GPUs get each piece, with Q4_K, MXFP4 and hybrid recurrent models as the gaps; three positionings with evidence, maintenance and names, and what a GitHub rename breaks; an upstream order with reviewers' concerns and an upstream-friendly split; recommended: a Pascal and mining-rig fork for any model with Flint as flagship, the docs repository's name kept, `pascal-p106*` frozen, a `pascal` release branch and `pascal-vN` tags).
